§ 01ApproachFour moves

I work the same way across every problem, whether the subject is an electricity grid, a food system or a procurement reform.

Establish what is actually known and where the evidence stops. Map the system and the actors inside it. Run country and comparative analysis. Narrow to a defensible set of strategic choices.

I am comfortable with fragmented, contradictory and incomplete data, and with problems where the right question has not yet been settled. That last condition is the common one, and treating it as the first piece of work rather than an obstacle is most of the method.

Alongside this I bring a working analytical toolkit, Python, R, SQL, statistical modelling, machine learning and Power BI, which lets me interrogate primary data directly rather than relying on secondary literature alone, and build analysis that outlives the engagement that produced it.

The household sits inside a system it did not design

This is the explanatory model behind the Tema Central energy study: household electricity behaviour connected to tariffs, appliance stock, income, weather and grid conditions at once, with every one of those arrows set by decisions taken at policy level. Select any node to trace what it touches.

The model

Six forces, one behaviour

Household electricity use is usually described as a habit. It is better described as a rational response to six conditions the household does not control. Studying any one of them alone explains very little, which is why the study integrated appliance survey data, consumption records, tariff schedules, meteorological data and energy-sector information into a single picture.

Applied research study · Tema Central, Ghana

Household energy consumption and demand-side management

Urban Ghanaian context · mixed-source analysis

01
  • Investigated household electricity consumption and energy-management behaviour, examining how household characteristics, appliance ownership, tariff structures and weather conditions interact to shape demand.
  • Integrated appliance survey data, consumption records, tariff schedules, meteorological data and energy-sector information into a single analytical picture, working across sources of differing quality and coverage.
  • Applied systems thinking to connect household-level behaviour to the wider technological, economic, environmental and policy conditions of Ghana's electricity system.
  • Built an analytical framework for evaluating household energy use and assessing demand-side interventions, producing evidence for decisions on efficiency and sustainable energy use.

Core finding

The binding constraint was rarely technical. Households responded rationally to tariffs, appliance stock and information they did not have. The gap sat between how the energy system was designed and how people actually live inside it.

Five sources, one analytical pictureWhere the difficulty actually was

The modelling was never the hard part. Getting five sources that were never designed to be joined into one usable frame was, and that work is why the rest of the study was possible at all.
